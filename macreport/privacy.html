Your Mac. Your data.

Updated 28 October 2026.

Device data and commands

MacReport uses the private CloudKit database in your Apple iCloud account to connect your devices. It stores system readings, a seven-day sampled history, the latest command and response, an event and command log, requested app lists and icons, notification thresholds and quiet-hour preferences, and cleanup plans, recovery metadata and results. History and event logs are trimmed as the running agent updates them; an offline agent cannot remove previously stored records. The developer does not run a separate telemetry server or use an advertising or analytics SDK in the apps.

Screen viewing

Screen viewing is off by default and requires permission and a local toggle on your Mac. Requested images are uploaded to your private iCloud, without audio. The iPhone accepts the requested frame for two minutes and discards it when the viewer closes or backgrounds. The running, online Mac agent removes the last cloud image on expiry, disable or lock. This is not a server-enforced deletion timer; an offline or closed agent cannot perform that deletion.

Direct Live screen

When you choose Live, your devices exchange short-lived connection details through your private iCloud. A WebRTC connection then carries screen frames and control messages directly between the Mac and iPhone, protected by DTLS encryption, without audio or recording. Live frames and input are not stored in CloudKit. MacReport does not configure a developer-operated relay by default. Google public STUN servers receive network connection information to help the devices find each other; they do not receive screen contents. Connection details can contain network addresses and remain in your private iCloud until replaced.

Live viewing requires Screen Recording permission, the local screen-viewing switch and command reception on an awake, unlocked Mac. Viewing can start without Accessibility permission or mouse and keyboard control. Control is a separate choice and requires the local control switch and Accessibility permission. Closing or backgrounding the iPhone viewer closes the connection and clears its image. Locking, sleep, revoked viewing permission, missing viewer heartbeats or the fifteen-minute session limit stop viewing. Control turns off after two idle minutes; viewing may continue. Reconnection does not replay input commands.

Optional owner-configured relay

Some networks block direct connections. The owner can configure a separate TURN server in the Mac agent. Its master authentication secret stays in this Mac’s Keychain; only temporary credentials, valid for twenty minutes, are shared through the owner’s private iCloud. A configured relay carries WebRTC-encrypted traffic and can observe network addresses and traffic volume. Its operator and hosting provider process that network information under their own policies. MacReport does not provision a relay or enable a provider automatically. Requested iCloud images remain available as a separate fallback.

Opening apps and screen control

The agent can report installed application names, bundle identifiers and small application icons through your private iCloud and open an application chosen from that report. Screen control requires a separate local opt-in, Screen Recording and Accessibility permission on the Mac. In the requested-image mode, clicks, scroll coordinates, fixed keys and execution results use the private command slot. In Live they use the direct encrypted connection. Sessions expire after two idle minutes, with a fifteen-minute maximum; a locked or sleeping Mac does not accept input.

Text is encrypted for the Mac before upload using system cryptography. The device-local private key stays in the Mac login Keychain; the iPhone obtains its public key through your private iCloud. Typed text is not stored in telemetry diagnostics or history and does not use the Mac clipboard. The encrypted text envelope is removed from execution and final acknowledgements. An undelivered encrypted command may remain in the cloud slot until replacement or agent processing. The entry sheet clears its local buffer on send, cancel or background. Text visible inside the controlled application can appear in requested screen images.

Nearby controller

The Nearby controller is a separate, optional trackpad and action panel for devices on the same local network. It is off by default on the Mac. First pairing shares a random authentication key through your private iCloud database; cached pairing keys stay in the device Keychain for up to 30 days. Disabling the local controller on your Mac immediately stops its listener and revokes the key. Removal of the cloud pairing record also requires the agent to be online. Re-enabling creates a new key.

After pairing, pointer movement, scrolling, fixed keys, typed text and supported brightness or audio actions travel directly over a TLS-encrypted local connection, without a cloud command queue. The local service advertises a non-personal identifier, not its authentication key. The developer does not receive these inputs. Mouse and keyboard input requires the Mac's separate control switch and Accessibility permission; Screen Recording is not required for this controller. A locked or sleeping Mac does not accept input. Control stops after two idle minutes and connections last at most fifteen minutes. Reconnecting requires a new explicit control session and does not replay input.

Cleanup

Analysis runs locally on the Mac. It inspects supported app, browser and developer caches, old logs and diagnostic reports, plus accessible Downloads, Desktop, Documents and Movies folders for installers, large and old files, and exact duplicates. A bounded storage breakdown covers accessible user folders. Scans report incomplete or unavailable locations and do not establish a complete index of the disk. File contents may be read locally to compare exact duplicates with SHA-256; cleanup does not upload file contents or content hashes. Names, paths, sizes, dates, scan coverage, retained-original paths and recommendations are sent to your own devices through private iCloud. Personal files are never selected automatically. The app asks you to review exact items before acting. With recovery enabled, selected data moves into a private local MacReport recovery folder. This move does not free disk space. You can restore an item only if its original location is free, or separately confirm permanent deletion. Recovery data is not purged automatically. Permanent deletion cannot be undone. An ordinary app-quit request may require confirmation for unsaved work on the Mac. Cloud files, symbolic and hard links, Photos libraries, application packages, recognized source projects, Xcode archives and simulator data are excluded from removal suggestions. Open files, active app caches and changed items cannot be cleaned until they pass a new check. A detailed plan allows up to fifteen minutes for review; the agent revalidates each chosen item before acting.

Notifications and local storage

CloudKit and Apple Push Notification service deliver configured event alerts. Apple Watch notification delivery follows your device settings. Readings, downloaded history, event logs and app catalogues are cached locally with their original timestamps; the Mac keeps local telemetry diagnostics and history. An optional stale-data reminder is scheduled locally and does not establish that the Mac is offline.

Emergency protection

When enabled in MacReport settings, this action stops remote sessions, attempts to lock the Mac and asks ordinary user applications to quit. It does not force quit apps or stop system services. The Mac stores a per-step result locally and shares it with your own devices through private iCloud, including app names and quit outcomes. Locking and app closure are reported separately; an app may need a local answer about unsaved work. Enabling this option does not run it. Starting requires a deliberate hold and separate confirmation.

Updates and this website

The Mac agent supports automatic checks and background downloading and installation of signed updates through Sparkle. These options are enabled by default for new installations and can be changed in the Mac agent’s settings. Existing explicit preferences are preserved. Sparkle may request an agent restart to finish an update; you can also check manually. The web host may receive the network address and ordinary request information needed to serve a page or download. This product page has no analytics script or advertising tracker. The page uses your browser language to select English or Russian, with English as the fallback. A manual language or appearance choice is stored in this browser’s local storage; these choices are not sent to the waitlist service. If you join the early-access waitlist, the separate waitlist service described below receives your email. Sparkle update archives must be authenticated with the application's update-signing key.

Early-access waitlist

If you submit the form, Oleg Yakushin stores your email address, signup date and the version of your consent to receive a MacReport invitation and early-access updates. This list is separate from app data in your private iCloud. Joining the list does not give the developer access to your Mac, iCloud or screen.

The waitlist service is hosted through OpenAI Sites with Cloudflare D1 storage. These providers also process ordinary request information needed to deliver and secure the service. Subscriber addresses are available only to the owner after sign-in and can be exported privately to send invitations. This form does not send an automatic email or invitation.

We use the address for MacReport invitations and early-access updates, not advertising for other products. You can withdraw your consent or ask to have your address removed by emailing hello@olegotka.es. Addresses are retained while the early-access list is in use or until you ask for removal.

Your choices

You can disable remote control, screen sharing, notifications and automatic update checks in the apps or system settings. Quitting the Mac agent stops new collection and command processing. Apple manages the iCloud and CloudKit infrastructure under your account settings. For questions, contact the developer through olegotka.es.
