Rebuild your travel history before counting the days.

Old trips can be scattered across emails, calendars and boarding passes. Gather the dates in one record, keep track of what you checked and leave uncertain dates visibly unresolved.

Collect the record one trip at a time

  1. Choose the period you need to check. Write down its start and end. An empty calendar does not tell you whether you were away or have not entered the trip yet.
  2. Find possible trips. Search booking emails, calendar entries, boarding passes and your own notes. Use them as clues to build a list.
  3. Confirm what actually happened. A reservation may have been changed or cancelled. Record actual arrival and departure dates separately from planned travel. Keep a short source note so you can revisit a date.
  4. Mark gaps. If you know a trip happened but cannot confirm its dates, write “needs checking”. Do not turn a guess into a confirmed date just to finish the table.
  5. Review the whole period. Sort trips by date and check for duplicates, missing departures and overlapping entries. Keep future plans separate from completed trips.

Use the dates with the right calculation

This template organises travel records; it does not calculate an authorised stay. For Schengen short stays, read the European Commission’s calculator instructions and check which rules apply to your documents and circumstances.
